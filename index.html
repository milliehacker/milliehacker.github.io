I'm a 2nd-year PhD student in Linguistics at Michigan State University and a research assistant for the Sociolinguistics Lab and its associated corpus project, the MI Diaries. I'm also an active member of the Phonology & Phonetics Group.

Research

I'm interested in phonological and phonetic variation, especially on the intra-speaker level. I am currently examining the structure and stylistic tendencies of narrative speech (spontaneous story-telling), which I believe will clarify the ramifications of narrative elicitation as a sociolinguistic method. The project feeds into a broader theme of determining how the sociolinguistic monitor operates across distinct modes or genres of speech.

My main backburner interests are: 1) phonetic signatures of syllabic affiliation, 2) the relationship between reduction and deletion, and 3) serial representations in Optimality Theory. To those ends, I've spent time in the past exploring C-Center stability, debuccalization and prefix deletion in Hixkaryana, hiatus resolution in Ojibwe, Super-Optimality, and Harmonic Serialism.
